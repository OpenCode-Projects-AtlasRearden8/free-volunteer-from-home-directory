# Archive Transcription

Turn handwritten letters, old military records, diaries, and scanned forms
into typed, searchable text. Libraries, museums, and archives worldwide hold
millions of scanned pages that no one can search until someone types them
out. Volunteers do exactly that, one line at a time.

## Who this suits

People who enjoy quiet, focused work; puzzle-solvers (old handwriting is a
puzzle); history fans; anyone who wants a project they can put down and pick
up again without losing their place. You never talk to anyone if you don't
want to.

## Time commitment

- **Minimum session:** 20–30 minutes is plenty. Most projects work a page or
  a field at a time, so short sessions are fine.
- **Typical volunteer:** 1–2 hours a week.
- **Note:** There is no schedule and no obligation. Transcribe one page or
  fifty — both count.

## Age notes

- Most major programs expect volunteers who can comfortably read cursive;
  in practice that usually means about **13–14+ with a parent's OK**, and
  **16–18+** for the platforms that require an account with age minimums.
- Younger kids can still join the fun with a grown-up doing the account and
  typing while a parent and child puzzle out tricky words together.
- No credentials or résumé needed — no proofreading experience required,
  everything is reviewed by more than one volunteer before it is finalized.

## Signing-up steps

1. Pick a project that fits where you live or what interests you. Well-known
   examples include the **Smithsonian Digital Volunteers Transcription
   Center**, the **Library of Congress' "By the People"** crowdsourcing
   campaign, and state or local archives with their own transcription
   portals — a web search for "library transcription volunteers" plus your
   state or country will find plenty.
2. Create a free account on the project's site (username and email — that is
   all any of these ask for).
3. Complete any short orientation or tutorial screen the site offers (most
   take five minutes or less).
4. Pick a one-page document marked as "needs transcription."
5. Read the site's style instructions (how to mark unclear words, line
   breaks, and dates) — following them matters more than typing fast.
6. Transcribe. Submit. That's it. Another volunteer reviews your page, and
   it becomes searchable history.

## Tips from long-time transcribers

- Start with typed or printed documents rather than fancy cursive
  handwriting; you learn the interface without fighting the penmanship.
- If a word is unreadable, mark it per instructions (usually
  `[unclear]`) and move on — never invent a guess that "sounds right."
- Set a timer for 30 minutes. Archival sprints work better than heroic
  marathons.
