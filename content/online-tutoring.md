# Online Tutoring

Help a student get unstuck in algebra, chemistry, essay structure, or SAT
math — one 30-minute session at a time, from your kitchen table. Programs
like **UPchieve** (free tutoring for low-income middle and high schoolers in
the U.S.) match volunteer coaches with students who request help in real
time; other small programs and through school partnerships offer similar
roles.

## Who this suits

Patient explainers. You don't need to be an expert — you need to be 8th
grade, 9th grade, or high-school-capable in your subject and kind while
explaining. People who remember what it felt like to be confused.

## Time commitment

- **Minimum session:** a single 30-minute session.
- **Typical volunteer:** 1 hour a week (two sessions) is a very normal
  cadence.
- On-demand platforms show you a queue; you accept students when you're
  online. That means there are no "no-shows" in your calendar — you simply
  log on when you have 30 minutes free.

## Age notes

- Generally **18+** (or the platform's stated minimum), because you're
  interacting with minors and platforms screen for the students' safety; a
  background check is standard.
- College students make wonderful tutors and are often explicitly
  recruited.
- Subject knowledge is validated by a quick proficiency quiz (for example,
  a 10-question algebra quiz), not by a teaching credential.

## Signing-up steps

1. Pick a platform — e.g. **UPchieve** for U.S. middle/high-school support,
   or search "free online tutoring volunteer" plus your subject and country.
2. Create your volunteer account (name, email, availability window).
3. Pass the subject proficiency quiz(es) for the subject(s) you want to
   tutor — retakes are typically allowed.
4. Complete the short training/orientation module (how sessions work, what
   to do if a student shares something worrying, how to report concerns
   through the platform rather than handling them yourself).
5. Complete any background-check step.
6. Go online when you want. Accept a request. Help one student.

## What a first session actually feels like

A student appears with a specific problem: "I don't get slope." You ask them
to show what they tried, gently find the wrong turn, and work the next one
together. Fifteen minutes later they say "oh wait, that's it?" and leave. It
is the best feeling in this entire directory.

## Tips

- Ask "can you walk me through what you did?" before correcting anything.
  Half the time they talk their way into the fix.
- Never give the answer. Guide to it. (Platforms train this too.)
- One subject, one time slot a week to start. Add more only if you look
  forward to it.
